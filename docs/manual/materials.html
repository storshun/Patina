Materials

A material is a complete surface: color, roughness, metallic, height (bumps) and emission, all at once. Patina paints materials. You don't paint each map on its own.

The Patina default library

Patina ships with 270 materials. All of them are made by Patina's own procedural generators, so they tile seamlessly at any resolution and carry no third-party licenses. Their ids all start with patina_ (for example patina_rust_heavy), so they never clash with your own materials.

Patina renders physically based materials, so the Character and Stylized sets get their look from the textures themselves: clean colors, soft or hard-edged tone steps, light and shadow painted into the base color, and little or no relief. Hair runs along the V direction: paint it with UV projection on hair cards, or turn the material until the strands follow the hair. For a stylized finish on any of them, add the Stylized edge highlights smart material on top.

Opacity, sheen, clearcoat and subsurface

Besides color, roughness, metallic, height and glow, a material has four more properties (Patina 0.26):

PropertyWhat it is for
Opacity1 is solid; lower lets what is behind show through. Lace, fishnet, leaves and hair cards cut holes with it, chiffon and glass are partly see-through.
SheenA soft light at grazing angles, the way velvet, satin, fur and peach-skin catch the light.
ClearcoatA smooth varnish over the surface: car paint, lacquered wood, nail polish, polished stone. It follows the shape, not the bumps under it.
SubsurfaceLight that goes into the material and comes out softer and warmer: skin, wax, leaves, jade, marble.

The skin, satin, velvet, car paint, marble and hair materials of the library have them now, and many of the new materials use them. You paint them like the other properties: a brush paints them where its material has them, a fill layer brings them with its material, and every layer has an Opacity, Sheen, Clearcoat and Subsurface channel (see Layers). The base material below all layers has them too. The 3D view shows them in the Material view, and the view list has a view of each. Exports write their maps only when the texture set uses them (see Exporting), and Blender gets them through the live link.

Cut out holes. Generators with holes (net, lace, leaves, hair cards, velvet's devoré, perforated metal, grilles, chainmail) have a Cut out holes tick box in the editor's Surface section: on, the holes are see-through; off, they show color C. The library's lace, nets, leaf cards, ivy and hair cards have it on.

Hair cards and leaf cardsThese are made to be used with UV mapping on cards (flat strips of faces): the strands run down the card with their roots at the top, and one leaf fills the card.

Use the search field to search by name, category, description or tag (try weathered, floor or hard surface). Click a material to paint with it. Right-click it to paint with it, add it as a fill layer, or duplicate it into the editor. Drag it onto a fill layer to give that layer the material, between layers for a new fill layer there, or onto the model for a new fill layer above the selected one (see Drag and drop). The panel's other tabs hold the Smart materials and the Smart masks, which drag into the stack the same way.

The two buttons under the search field show the materials as tiles or as a list. The list has a small picture, the name, and the category and library of each one, so you can read long names and scan more of them at once. In the tile view, the Size slider sets how big the pictures are, and so does Ctrl+mouse wheel over them. The tiles always fit the panel's width. Long names are cut with "...", and the tooltip shows them whole. The Smart materials tab uses the same view, and Patina remembers it.

Material settings

When you paint, the material is projected onto the model. Properties › Paint material (and the same block in a fill layer's settings) controls how:

SettingMeaning
ProjectionTri-planar (the default) projects from three directions in object space, so it is seamless and ignores UVs. UV follows the UVs, which suits labels or anything that must follow the layout. Screen projects the image from the camera, like a decal or a stencil.
Scale / RotationThe pattern size and its angle.
TintMultiplies the color. One rust can become many.
Rough scale / offsetMakes the material glossier or duller.
Height scale, Normal strengthHow strong the bumps are. Set 0 to paint color without relief.

Your own materials

Import a scanned material

Use File › Import Material Folder…, or drop a folder onto the window. Patina recognizes the usual file names from ambientCG, Poly Haven, Quixel and Substance exports (*_Color, *_BaseColor, *_Roughness, *_Metalness, *_NormalGL / *_NormalDX, *_Displacement, *_AO, *_Emission…). The material is copied into your library, so the original folder can be deleted. The status bar says how many maps were found, and the Console lists which file each channel came from.

Materials from Substance

Textures exported from Substance 3D Designer, Painter or Sampler, and materials downloaded from Substance 3D Assets as images, import directly, whichever export preset was used:

What is in the folderWhat Patina does
Separate maps (Document channels, PBR Metallic Roughness)Each file feeds its channel.
*_OcclusionRoughnessMetallic (Unreal preset), *_ORM, *_ARMSplits the packed file: green is roughness, blue is metallic, red is ambient occlusion.
*_metallicRoughness (glTF)Green is roughness, blue is metallic.
*_MaskMap (Unity HDRP), *_MetallicSmoothness (Unity URP / Standard)Red is metallic, the alpha channel is smoothness (turned into roughness), the mask map's green is occlusion.
*_BaseMap, *_AlbedoTransparencyBase color.
Several texture sets in one folder (Robot_Body_BaseColor.png, Robot_Head_BaseColor.png…)One material per texture set, named after it (Robot - Body, Robot - Head).
EXR, HDR and 16-bit PNG mapsRead at full precision. A height map in scene units (an EXR displacement) is fitted into Patina's height range, and linear EXR color is converted to sRGB.

Normal maps: a name that says OpenGL, DirectX, GL or DX decides the convention. A plain *_Normal next to an OcclusionRoughnessMetallic map comes from Substance's Unreal preset, so it is read as DirectX. Anything else is read as OpenGL. If the bumps look like dents, right-click the material › Normal map is DirectX.

.sbsar files can't be read. An .sbsar is a compiled Substance Designer graph, and only Adobe's Substance engine can render one. Open it in Substance Designer, Sampler or Player, export its outputs as images, and import that folder. The same goes for Painter's own files (.spsm smart materials, .spmsk smart masks, .spp projects): export the textures, or rebuild the stack with Patina's smart materials. Patina says so when you drop one of those files.

Images and decals

Drop a single image onto the viewport and it becomes a color-only material with Screen projection. That makes it a stencil: aim the view, then paint the logo or photo onto the model. Drop it onto the Brushes panel instead and it becomes a brush tip.

The material editor

New procedural…, or Edit / duplicate… on any library material, opens the editor. Pick a generator (there are 74, grouped by kind, from rust and wood to diamond plate, sci-fi panels, skin, hand-painted stone, prints and stained glass), set its colors and values, and watch the live sphere and tile at the top of the window update. Save to library stores it under your user folder.

Each generator's colors and settings are named for what they do: Brick, Second brick and Mortar for bricks, then Joint width, Bond, Wear and Length : height. A color the generator doesn't use is hidden. A setting with a choice of styles (a brick bond, a stripe style, an animal) is a drop-down. Where 0 stands for a default, the slider says default and the value it uses. Claude reads the same descriptions with list_generators.

Wear and wobble

Three settings work with every generator, at the foot of the editor:

SettingDoes
Grime, Grime colorDirt settles in the low parts of the pattern: the grout, the mortar, the gaps between planks, cracks and grooves. They also get rougher, and a thin film covers the rest.
Worn edges, Edge colorThe raised edges of the pattern wear to the edge color and get a little smoother: chipped brick arrises, polished tread, rubbed-through paint.
Warp, Warp sizeWobbles the whole pattern: wavy stripes, bricks laid by hand, hand-drawn checks.

All three keep the material seamless. They work from the generator's own height, so a pattern with no relief (a flat print) has no grooves to fill and no edges to wear.

Gotchas

Built-in materials can't be changed in placeEditing a P material creates a copy called user_patina_… in the User category, and the original stays as it was. That is deliberate: your projects keep working when Patina is updated.
Old projects and the patina_ prefixProjects from Patina 0.1 refer to materials without the prefix (rust_heavy). They still open, because the old ids are mapped to the patina_… materials automatically.
Normal maps from other toolsSome materials come with DirectX-style normal maps (green channel flipped) whose names don't say so. If the bumps of an imported material look inverted, right-click it in the Materials panel › Normal map is DirectX. Claude and the API can pass "normal": "directx" to import_material_folder.
An imported material has no roughness or metalThe packed map has a name Patina doesn't know. Check the Console for the list of files it used, and rename the packed file so it ends in _ORM (red occlusion, green roughness, blue metallic) or _MaskMap, then import again.
Tri-planar on curved shapesTri-planar projection meets in a seam where a surface faces diagonally. Since 0.7.4 the seams are by height: at each point, the projection whose surface stands highest wins, so the transition follows the pattern (a raised tread lozenge stays whole) and nothing shows through. Older versions cross-faded the projections, which showed two patterns at half strength (ghosting), worst with sharp repeating patterns such as diamond plate, tiles or grilles. You can still pick Smooth cross-fade under Adjust › Tri-planar seams, which hides seams in noisy materials such as rust or concrete. Tri-planar blend sets how far from the diagonal the hand-over starts. For a regular pattern that must run unbroken around a curved part, use UV projection on a model with clean UVs, for example an island made with the Island brush.
