Painting

Choose a layer, a material and a brush, then paint on the model. This page explains what happens under the brush, so the results are predictable.

How painting works

Most painters stamp the brush into the texture image, so the stroke follows the UV layout and breaks at every seam. Patina works the other way round. For every texel (texture pixel) of the model it knows the point on the surface the texel belongs to. Each brush dab asks every texel the same question: is your surface point under the brush, and is it visible?

The result is that a stroke covers exactly what you see under the cursor, wherever the texels live in the texture. Seams don't break it, islands at different scales don't stretch it, and rotated or mirrored islands don't twist it. Materials are sampled in 3D as well, using tri-planar projection, so a wood grain or tile pattern lines up across seams too.

Tools

Brush settings

The viewport bar holds the settings you change most: size, hardness, flow, opacity, spacing, projection mode and the topology and island locks. When the viewport is narrow, the bar continues on a second row. The Properties panel has all of them.

SettingMeaning
SizeDiameter in screen pixels, which is what you see. In Surface 3D mode it is a size in object units.
Hardness0 gives a soft, feathered edge. 1 gives a crisp edge.
FlowHow much each dab adds. Low flow builds up gradually, like an airbrush.
OpacityThe ceiling for a whole stroke. No matter how often you go over an area within one stroke, it never exceeds this.
SpacingThe distance between dabs as a fraction of the diameter. Use small values for smooth lines and large values for stamps.
TipThe brush shape. There are 16 procedural tips (round, square, star, splatter, chalk, scratches, grunge, cracks…), or Image tip… loads any grayscale image.
Angle / RoundnessRotation and squash of the tip. Follow path (under Dynamics) turns the tip along the stroke.
DynamicsSize, angle and flow jitter, scatter, lazy mouse (the brush trails behind the cursor for smooth lines), and what pen pressure controls.

The Brushes tab has 27 presets in six groups: Basic, Artistic, Weathering, Stamps, Projection and Utility. Save preset in Properties stores the current settings as your own preset.

Your own brushes

Every image in your brushes folder is a brush (1.2): PNG, JPEG, TGA, BMP, GIF, PSD or EXR. Each folder inside it is a group of its own in the Brushes panel, and your groups come first. A Photoshop brush file (.abr) there gives a brush for each of its tips. Patina looks at the folder every few seconds, so a file you add shows up by itself, and one you remove goes.

  • Import brushes... at the top of the Brushes panel copies images or .abr files into the folder. So does dropping them on the panel.
  • Open the folder beside it opens the folder itself. It is ~/.local/share/patina/user/brushes on Linux and %LOCALAPPDATA%\Patina\user\brushes on Windows.

Patina reads a tip the way it was made. A dark shape on a light ground paints with the dark part, as Photoshop, GIMP and Krita tips are made. A light shape on a dark ground paints with the light part. An image with transparency paints with its opaque part. A brush's tip and size can be changed and saved as a preset like any other. Agents use import_brushes and reload_brushes.

Projection modes

ModeUse it for
Screen (default)Projects the brush from the view, like Substance Painter. You get what you see, which suits most work.
Surface 3DA sphere brush that sits on the surface. Its size is set in object space, so it stays the same however you zoom, and it wraps around corners.
UVDabs are placed in texture space. Use this for decals that should follow the UV layout.

Under Projection you also find Occlusion. With it off (or with the paint_through preset), the brush also reaches surfaces hidden behind others. Backface culling and Angle falloff fade the paint out on surfaces that turn away from you.

Smudge and seams

Smudge (J) picks up the paint under the brush and drags it along the stroke. It works in 3D like the other tools: the drag follows the surface, so it isn't stretched by the UV layout, and it runs on into the next island across a seam. Every channel the brush paints is smudged, so height and roughness move with the color. Flow sets how much paint each step carries (about 0.5 smears, 1 pushes the paint along with the brush), and pen pressure can control it under Dynamics. Symmetry copies each smudge their own way.

Blur and smudge carry on across UV seams: where an island ends at a seam, they read the matching texels of the neighboring island, or of the other edge of the texture when an island wraps around (a cylinder, the sphere's seam). So a blur along a seam leaves no hard line. Across UV seams in Properties turns this off. Patina works out the seams once per UV layout (in well under a second at 2K).

Stencils, decals and text

A stencil is a picture laid over the 3D view. It stays where it is on the screen while you turn the model, so you can line it up, and then the brush paints through it. Load one under Properties › Stencil & text (Image…), or make one from text.

SettingWhat it does
Mode: MaskThe stencil only shapes the paint: white and opaque parts let it through, black or transparent parts block it. You paint the brush material through it, like spray paint through a cut-out.
Mode: DecalThe stencil's own colors are painted, with roughness, metallic and the other channels from the brush material. Use it for logos, labels and photos.
Invert · TilePaint where the stencil is dark instead · repeat it across the whole view (patterns, mesh, perforations).
Position · Scale · RotationOr hold T in the view: drag to move it, Shift+drag to rotate it, the wheel to scale it. Fit centers it.
Overlay · ShowHow strongly the stencil is drawn over the view. Hiding it doesn't switch it off.
Project onto the modelPaints the whole stencil in one step onto everything the camera sees, with the brush's occlusion, backface and angle settings. Erase through it erases instead. One undo step.

Painting through a stencil works with Paint and Erase, in the 3D view, in all three projection modes. Shift+T turns the stencil on and off.

Text

Under Stencil & text › Text, type one or more lines and press Make text stencil. Patina renders the text into a stencil image (decal mode when the color isn't white), which you place and paint like any other stencil. With Live on, edits to the text update the stencil straight away.

  • Fonts: four are included (DejaVu Sans, Sans Bold, Serif and Sans Mono), and the list also shows the fonts installed on the computer. File… uses any .ttf or .otf file.
  • Size, color, outline (width and color), alignment, letter and line spacing.
  • Save image… writes the stencil as a PNG, for example to use the text as an image material or brush tip.
  • Claude sets all of this with set_stencil (text, font, font_size, outline…) and paints it with project_stencil; list_fonts lists the fonts.

Topology lock and island lock

Two toggles on the viewport bar keep a stroke where it started. They work with the Paint, Erase, Blur, Smudge and Clone tools, in every projection mode, and in the UV editor's paint mode.

ToggleKeyA stroke only paints…
Topology lockAlt+Tthe connected piece of mesh it starts on. Pieces that are separate in the mesh (eyes, buttons, straps, a separate lid) stay clean, even where they touch or overlap on screen.
Island lockAlt+Lthe UV island it starts on. The stroke stops at the island's seams, so you can paint one panel of a hard-surface model, or one side of a box, right up to the edge.
Set lock(none)the texture set it starts on, or, with a single texture, its material slot: the skin, not the collar next to it. Shown when the model has several sets or slots.

The region is chosen by the first dab that lands on the model, so you can start a stroke beside the model and drag onto it. With symmetry on, the mirrored part is unlocked as well, so both sides get painted. With both toggles on, the island lock decides (an island never spans two separate pieces of mesh). The toggles are remembered between sessions, and Claude can set them with set_brush (lock_topology, lock_island, lock_set).

Colors

When the brush paints a flat color (no library material selected), the Colors panel next to Properties is the place to choose it.

  • Primary and secondary: the large square is the color you paint with. X (or Swap) swaps it with the secondary color, like the foreground and background colors in an image editor.
  • Picker: a full color picker with a hex field. Choosing a color here switches the brush from a library material to flat color.
  • Recent: the last 16 colors you painted with or picked from the model.
  • Swatches: your own palette. + adds the primary color. Right-click a swatch to replace it, use it as the secondary color or remove it.

Swatches, recent colors and the secondary color are kept between sessions, together with the brush, the material, the tool and the symmetry settings.

Lines, symmetry and pressure

  • Straight lines: paint once, then Shift+click somewhere else. Patina draws a line from the end of the last stroke.
  • Mirror symmetry: Mirror X / Y / Z in the tool strip mirror every dab across the object's axes. They combine: X and Z together paint four copies, all three paint eight. Shift+X toggles X. The copies paint the other side even when the camera can't see it: a copy that is out of view or facing away goes straight onto the model's surface.
  • Radial symmetry: Radial in the tool strip repeats every dab 2–32 times around the X, Y or Z axis, for wheels, rosettes, bolts around a flange and so on. It works together with the mirrors. All copies go through the object's origin, so the model should be centered on it.
  • Pen pressure: works with drawing tablets on Wayland, X11 and Windows. By default pressure controls size; flow and opacity are optional under Dynamics. See Pen tablets.

Pen tablets

Patina reads drawing tablets (Wacom, Huion, XP-Pen and others) directly. It uses every sample the tablet sends, typically 130 to 266 per second, so fast strokes keep their shape and their pressure, independent of the frame rate.

FeatureWhat it does
PressureSize, flow and/or opacity, chosen per brush under Properties › Dynamics. Mouse strokes always paint at full pressure, even right after using the pen.
Pressure curveFeel (softer: light touches already paint strongly; firmer: press harder), Ignore below (pressure that counts as none, for pens that register when they only rest on the tablet) and Full pressure at (so a light hand still reaches full size). In Edit › Preferences › Pen tablet and the test window.
Eraser endTurn the pen around and it erases, whatever the tool. Switch it off with The eraser end erases.
Barrel buttonsEach button can be a right click, a middle click (which orbits or pans, depending on the navigation preset), swap colors, undo, or nothing. The defaults are right click (lower button) and middle click (upper button).
TiltPen tilt turns the tip (under Dynamics) points non-round tips the way the pen leans. Needs a pen with tilt (Wacom Pro Pen, most Huion and XP-Pen pens; the standard Intuos pen has none).

Checking a tablet

  1. Open Help › Pen Tablet Test… (also Pen tablet settings… under Dynamics, and Test the pen… in Preferences).
  2. Hover over the window: the status line should say Pen: hovering. Draw in the pad, pressing lightly, then hard. The bars show the raw pressure and the pressure after the curve, and the graph shows the last four seconds.
  3. Check the numbers: the Range seen should go from about 0 to close to 1. Samples per second while you move the pen should be about 100 or more. Turn the pen round to see Eraser seen, lean it to see the tilt.
  4. Set Feel until light and hard strokes feel right in the pad. The settings are kept between sessions.
  5. If something is off, Copy report puts a description of what Patina receives on the clipboard (the system, the event counts, the pressure range, the findings) for a bug report. Claude can read the same with the pen_info command.

Setting up the driver

Linux. Wacom tablets work through the kernel's wacom driver and libinput, with nothing to install. Huion tablets work through the kernel's hid-uclogic driver: most models get pressure on a current kernel such as Fedora's. GNOME (Settings › Wacom Tablet) and KDE (System Settings › Drawing Tablet) set the tablet area, the pen buttons and the pressure feel for the whole desktop; Patina's curve comes on top of that. To check what the system sees, install libinput-utils and run sudo libinput list-devices (the tablet should list the tablet capability) and sudo libinput debug-tablet (shows the pressure while you press).

Windows. Patina reads tablets through Windows Ink, so it must be enabled in the tablet driver: in Wacom Tablet Properties, the Mapping tab, tick Use Windows Ink; in the Huion Tablet app, under Digital Pen, tick Enable Windows Ink. Restart Patina after changing it. Patina doesn't use the older Wintab interface.

Channels

A paint stroke writes several channels at once: base color, roughness, metallic, height, normal and emissive, and opacity, sheen, clearcoat and subsurface when the brush's material has them (or the layer already has them, so the eraser, blur, smudge and clone reach them). A plain brush leaves those four alone. In the Brush panel's Paint channels you choose which channels strokes write. Unticking a channel in the layer's Channels row stops that layer affecting it. For example, a varnish layer might change only roughness. Each channel also has its own opacity under Channel opacity.

Gotchas

Paint appears in two places at onceTwo parts of the model share the same part of the texture. This is common with mirrored halves and with stacked islands, as in the DamagedHelmet sample. Painting one copy paints the other. To fix it, give the mesh unique UVs: UV › Unwrap › Smart UV Project, then Pack Islands. More…
“Nothing to paint”The selected layer is hidden, all its channels are unticked, or no layer is selected. Fill and adjustment layers paint their mask, which is invisible if the layer itself shows nothing yet.
Strokes look blockyThe texture resolution is too low for how close you're working, or that part of the UVs is small (check with the UV checker view). Raise the resolution in Texture Set. Your layers are resampled, not lost.
X no longer toggles symmetrySince 0.3, X swaps the primary and secondary color. The X mirror moved to Shift+X. To get the old key back, change it in Edit › Keyboard shortcuts.
Quick pick stays on the pickerA short tap of I without clicking selects the Pick tool and stays there (the old behavior). Only holding I, or clicking while it is held, returns to the previous tool when you let go.
“My stroke stops for no reason”The topology or island lock is on (the button is orange on the viewport bar). Check the status bar after Alt+T / Alt+L, or turn the Seams overlay on to see where islands end. If the topology lock stops at a place that looks connected, the mesh is split there (for example, vertices that were not merged in Blender).
The pen moves the cursor but there is no pressureThe tablet driver delivers the pen as a plain mouse. The pen test window then says No pen events yet or shows no pressure range. On Windows, turn on Windows Ink in the tablet driver (see Pen tablets). On Linux, check with sudo libinput debug-tablet. If that shows no pressure either, the kernel driver doesn't support the model yet. For Huion tablets that is usually fixed by a newer kernel, the DIGImend kernel drivers or OpenTabletDriver; use only one of them at a time, and quit Huion's own Linux driver if it is running.
Strokes start with a blob or a thin tailAdjust the curve: raise Ignore below if the pen paints while it barely touches, or set Feel firmer. The desktop's own pressure curve (GNOME / KDE / the Wacom or Huion settings) also applies, so set one of the two and leave the other linear.
Nothing gets painted with a stencil onIn mask mode only the white, opaque parts of the stencil let paint through, and nothing gets through outside the stencil (unless Tile or Invert is on). A black-on-transparent logo needs Invert or decal mode. Check that the stencil is over the part you are painting: it stays fixed on the screen when you turn the model.
Smudge pushes everything awayAt flow 1 the brush carries all the paint it touches to the end of the stroke. Lower the flow to about 0.5 for a smear that leaves a trail.
Painting through thin partsWith Occlusion off, paint goes all the way through: ears, fingers and both sides of a thin plate get painted. Use it on purpose, then turn it back on.
